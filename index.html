No.04 · Buyer Journey

Eight steps, clearly explained.

Every buyer is different, so the path is too. Choose the situation that matches yours, then open any step to see what happens, how Vali helps, and what to have ready.

What happens

We set out what you are trying to achieve, when you want to move, and what the realistic path looks like from where you are today.

How Vali helps

Vali maps the whole process in plain language before any paperwork, so you know the order of events and roughly what each stage costs.

What to prepare

A rough moving date, who is buying with you, and any non-negotiables such as school catchment or commute.

What happens

You get a mortgage pre-approval and we add the costs that sit on top of the purchase price.

How Vali helps

Vali introduces mortgage professionals, then walks through land transfer tax, legal fees, title insurance, inspection and moving costs, and which first-time buyer rebates you may qualify for.

What to prepare

Recent pay stubs, notices of assessment, and a clear picture of your savings and existing debts.

What happens

We turn your criteria into a live search and narrow the areas that actually fit your budget and daily life.

How Vali helps

Vali sets up MLS alerts and shares what he knows about each pocket: pricing patterns, what tends to sit, and what tends to move quickly.

What to prepare

An honest list of what you need versus what you would like, and your maximum commute.

What happens

You start viewing, and each showing teaches you more about what your budget buys in that area.

How Vali helps

Vali points out the things that cost money later: age of roof, furnace and windows, grading, layout limitations, and how the home is likely to resell.

What to prepare

A note-taking habit. Photos and a short list of concerns after each showing help far more than memory.

What happens

Before writing, we review comparable sales and decide which conditions belong in the offer.

How Vali helps

Vali explains what each clause does, what a condition protects you from, and how the deposit and closing date work.

What to prepare

Your lawyer's contact details, and a decision on how quickly you can close.

What happens

The offer goes in and the back-and-forth begins, sometimes against other buyers.

How Vali helps

Vali handles the negotiation and keeps you anchored to the number you set beforehand, so decisions are made on evidence rather than pressure in the moment.

What to prepare

A firm walk-away number agreed before the offer is presented, not during.

What happens

Once an offer is accepted, the conditions are worked through within the agreed deadlines.

How Vali helps

Vali coordinates the home inspection, lender appraisal and lawyer review, and explains anything the inspector flags so you can decide whether to proceed, renegotiate or walk.

What to prepare

Availability for the inspection, and prompt responses to your lender's document requests.

What happens

Conditions are waived, the file moves to the lawyers, and closing day is scheduled.

How Vali helps

Vali keeps the lawyer, lender and other side coordinated, and sends a moving checklist covering utilities, insurance and address changes.

What to prepare

Closing funds at your lawyer, home insurance bound, and movers booked once the deal is firm.

What happens

We define what the next home needs to solve that the current one does not.

How Vali helps

Vali works through space, location, schools and long-term plans, and is direct about which of those the market can realistically deliver in your range.

What to prepare

A clear list of what is driving the move, and your ideal timing.

What happens

We establish what your current property is likely to sell for and what equity that frees up.

How Vali helps

Vali prepares an evaluation from recent comparable sales on your street, and outlines what preparation would be worth doing before listing.

What to prepare

Your mortgage balance, any secured lines of credit, and a list of improvements you have made.

What happens

Your lender confirms what you can carry, including any overlap between the two transactions.

How Vali helps

Vali connects you with mortgage professionals who can explain porting, bridge financing and what qualifying looks like while you still hold the first property.

What to prepare

Current mortgage documents, renewal date, and whether your mortgage is portable.

What happens

We weigh the two sequences against your finances and your tolerance for uncertainty.

How Vali helps

Vali sets out the real trade-offs of each path in current conditions rather than a blanket rule, since the right answer changes with inventory and with your ability to carry both.

What to prepare

An honest view of how much risk you are comfortable carrying, and whether you have a fallback if timing slips.

What happens

Preparation on the current home runs alongside the search for the next one.

How Vali helps

Vali coordinates the prep work, photography and launch plan while keeping your search active, so neither side stalls waiting on the other.

What to prepare

Access for trades and photographers, and decisions on decluttering and any recommended repairs.

What happens

Offers are negotiated on the sale and the purchase, sometimes at the same time.

How Vali helps

Vali negotiates both sides with an eye on how they interact, since a concession on one can create or remove flexibility on the other.

What to prepare

Fast availability to review and respond, as both sides often move quickly.

What happens

Conditions and closing dates are aligned as closely as the two agreements allow.

How Vali helps

Vali works with both lawyers and lenders to line the dates up. Where a gap remains, he explains the options for bridging or storage early rather than late.

What to prepare

Flexibility on dates where possible, and a plan if the two closings do not land on the same day.

What happens

Both deals close and you hand over one property while taking possession of another.

How Vali helps

Vali keeps the sequence organised across lawyers, movers and utilities, and stays reachable through the handover.

What to prepare

Movers booked, utilities scheduled at both addresses, and keys and garage remotes gathered for handover.

What happens

We establish whether you are buying for monthly income, long-term appreciation, or a mix, and over what horizon.

How Vali helps

Vali is direct about what each objective demands in this market, and which property types tend to suit which goal.

What to prepare

Your target hold period, how involved you want to be, and what return would make this worthwhile.

What happens

Investment financing is assessed, which differs from financing a home you live in.

How Vali helps

Vali introduces lenders experienced with rental properties and explains typical down payment requirements, qualifying treatment of rental income, and why a reserve matters.

What to prepare

Corporate or personal structure decided with your accountant, plus proof of down payment and reserves.

What happens

We build a shortlist that fits the strategy rather than whatever happens to be listed.

How Vali helps

Vali screens for tenant demand, condition, zoning and the realistic rent for each unit, and rules out properties whose numbers do not hold up.

What to prepare

Your buy box: areas, property types, unit count and maximum price.

What happens

Each candidate is modelled on income and expenses rather than list price alone.

How Vali helps

Vali works through rent, vacancy allowance, taxes, insurance, maintenance, management and financing costs, and shows the cash flow those inputs produce.

What to prepare

Your financing terms, and any assumptions you want tested.

What happens

We rerun the numbers with worse inputs to see what the property does under strain.

How Vali helps

Vali models lower rent, higher vacancy and higher borrowing costs, so you can see where the deal stops working before you commit.

What to prepare

A view on how much negative cash flow, if any, you could absorb and for how long.

What happens

An offer is prepared with conditions suited to an income property.

How Vali helps

Vali structures the offer around the checks that matter here: inspection, review of leases and rent rolls, and confirmation of permitted use.

What to prepare

Your lawyer briefed on the purchase, and availability during the condition period.

What happens

The deal closes and the property has to start operating.

How Vali helps

Vali coordinates closing, and helps with the handover of existing tenancies or with getting the unit ready and leased where it is vacant.

What to prepare

Decision on self-managing or hiring a manager, plus insurance suited to a rental property.

What happens

After a period of operating, actual results are compared against the original model.

How Vali helps

Vali reviews where reality differed from the projection and what that means for refinancing, improving the property, or acquiring the next one.

What to prepare

Twelve months of income and expense records for the property.

What happens

We clarify whether this is for your own use, for rental, or for resale, and when you actually need it.

How Vali helps

Vali explains how pre-construction timelines work, including that occupancy and final closing are separate dates and that delays are common.

What to prepare

Your intended use, and the earliest and latest dates that would work for you.

What happens

Projects are compared on more than the renderings and the launch pricing.

How Vali helps

Vali reviews builder track records, past delivery timelines, construction quality and location fundamentals, and flags projects he would be cautious about.

What to prepare

Your preferred areas, and whether you want a condominium, townhome or freehold.

What happens

The purchase price is only part of what you will pay.

How Vali helps

Vali walks through the deposit schedule, development levies and whether they are capped, occupancy fees, assignment fees and closing costs, so the total is clear at the outset.

What to prepare

Confirmation of how much you can put down across the deposit schedule, not just at signing.

What happens

Within a project, specific units perform differently.

How Vali helps

Vali advises on floor plan, exposure, floor level and layout efficiency, since these affect both livability and how the unit resells or rents.

What to prepare

Your priorities on size, layout, parking and storage.

What happens

The agreement is reviewed during the statutory cooling-off period.

How Vali helps

Vali flags the clauses that matter, including assignment rights, levy caps and the builder's ability to change dates, and coordinates with your lawyer for the formal review.

What to prepare

A real estate lawyer engaged and available within the cooling-off window.

What happens

The project is built over a period of years while you prepare to complete.

How Vali helps

Vali passes on builder updates and reminds you of deposit dates, and works with your lender well ahead of closing since approval must be current at that time.

What to prepare

Deposit funds available on schedule, and finances kept stable ahead of final approval.

What happens

You inspect the unit before taking occupancy and note deficiencies.

How Vali helps

Vali explains the pre-delivery inspection, what to document, and how occupancy fees work in the interim period before final closing.

What to prepare

Time set aside for the inspection, and a careful record of anything incomplete or damaged.

What happens

Title transfers, your mortgage funds, and the unit becomes yours.

How Vali helps

Vali coordinates final closing with your lender and lawyer, and helps you decide whether to move in, lease it out or sell.

What to prepare

Final closing costs ready, and a decision on how you intend to use the unit.

No.11 · Common Questions

Answers, before you ask.

The questions that come up most often from buyers and sellers across Hamilton and Burlington. If yours is not here, call or send a note. Straight answers, no obligation.

Values vary widely by neighbourhood, lot size, condition, and what has actually sold on your street in the last ninety days. Online estimators miss renovations, exposure, and the demand in your specific pocket, which is where most of the difference lives. A free in-person evaluation reviews recent solds within a few blocks, the inventory you would be competing against, and the pricing strategy most likely to produce competing offers rather than a slow drift downward.

Days on market depends on price band, season, and preparation. Well-prepared homes priced to the market often sell within one to three weeks. Homes priced above market or listed without preparation can sit considerably longer, and every week of sitting costs leverage. Pre-market preparation, professional photography, and a launch that concentrates showings into the first weekend have the single largest effect on both speed and final price.

Budget roughly 1.5 to 4 percent of the purchase price. The main items are land transfer tax, legal fees and disbursements, title insurance, a home inspection, and adjustments for prepaid property taxes or utilities. First-time buyers may qualify for a land transfer tax rebate. Exact figures depend on the property and your lawyer, so confirm the specifics with your legal and mortgage professionals before you firm up.

You are not required to have one, but the sales centre staff work for the builder, not for you. Representation gets you platinum or early access allocations, a review of the agreement during the cooling-off period alongside your lawyer, attention to development levy caps and assignment terms that are easy to miss, and a realistic model of carrying costs at occupancy. Vali has sold over 350 pre-construction units since 2015.

The deposit submitted with an offer is separate from your down payment. It typically ranges from a few thousand dollars up to about five percent of the purchase price, depending on the local market and how competitive the offer needs to look. Separately, the minimum down payment in Canada is five percent on the first five hundred thousand dollars and ten percent on the portion above that, with twenty percent required to avoid mortgage default insurance.

Primary focus is Hamilton and Burlington, where the majority of transactions happen. Active coverage extends through Ancaster, Dundas, Stoney Creek, Grimsby, Waterdown, Oakville, Milton, Mississauga, and Toronto. If you are buying or selling anywhere along the western Golden Horseshoe or into the GTA, it is covered.

In most residential resale transactions in Ontario, the listing brokerage offers compensation to the co-operating brokerage out of the seller's proceeds, so buyers typically do not pay directly. Arrangements vary by transaction and are set out in a written buyer representation agreement before you start looking, so there are no surprises. Any exceptions are explained up front.
